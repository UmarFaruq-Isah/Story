1. Electrons in Metals

Characteristics of Electrons in Metals
Metals have many free (conduction) electrons.
These electrons move randomly in the absence of an electric field.
When a voltage is applied, the electrons move in the opposite direction to the electric field, creating an
electric current.
Electrical conductivity depends on the number of free electrons and their mobility.
Electron Drift Velocity
The average velocity attained by electrons under an applied electric field is called the drift velocity.
Electron Emission
Electron emission is the process by which electrons escape from the surface of a material after receiving
sufficient energy to overcome the work function.
Types of Electron Emission
(a) Thermionic Emission
Occurs when a metal is heated to a high temperature.
Heat provides enough energy for electrons to escape.
Applications
Vacuum tubes
Cathode ray tubes (CRT)
Electron microscopes
(b) Photoelectric Emission
